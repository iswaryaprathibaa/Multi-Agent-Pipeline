Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is an architecture that combines a large language model
with an external knowledge retrieval step. Instead of relying solely on knowledge baked into
model weights at training time, a RAG system retrieves relevant documents or passages from a
knowledge base at query time and passes them to the model as additional context. This reduces
hallucination, allows the model to cite sources, and lets organizations ground answers in
proprietary or frequently changing data without retraining the model.

A typical RAG pipeline has three stages: indexing (splitting documents into chunks and
embedding them into a vector store), retrieval (embedding the user's query and finding the
most similar chunks via nearest-neighbor search), and generation (prompting the LLM with the
retrieved chunks plus the original query). Chunk size, overlap, and the choice of embedding
model materially affect retrieval quality.

In enterprise search, RAG is increasingly used to let employees ask natural-language questions
over internal wikis, tickets, and documents, returning answers with citations back to the
source documents rather than a list of links to click through.
